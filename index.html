Explainable inspection-priority engine

Methodology (summary)

  1. Zones. The study area is divided into a simulated grid. Each zone has illustrative inputs (elevation, drainage index, impervious share, a historical flag).
  2. Flood susceptibility score (0–100, simulated). Rainfall (scenario), inverted elevation, drainage quality, impervious share and the historical flag are combined with fixed maximum points and capped at 100.
  3. Damage severity (0–100). Derived from confirmed road-damage reports near a zone, using each report's severity and duplicate confirmations. No confirmed reports → 0, shown as "no confirmed damage data".
  4. Road importance (0–100). Derived from real OpenStreetMap road classes near a zone (primary > secondary > tertiary).
  5. Priority Score. 0.40 × damage + 0.35 × flood + 0.25 × road importance. Each term is labelled real, simulated or missing in every explanation.

Real data (with source)

  • Public-facility locations and road geometry/names: OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
  • Base map tiles: OpenStreetMap standard tiles.
  • Coordinates you pick on the map or share via GPS (only with consent) are real device coordinates.

Simulated / illustrative data

  • Flood-risk zones and scores use a transparent illustrative formula — not observed or validated flood data.
  • Rainfall intensities in the scenario selector are illustrative values for comparison only.
  • Historical flood notes are sample narrative text, not a verified incident register.
  • AI image severity is a simulated demonstration, not a trained vision model.
  • Bundled Demo road-damage reports are fabricated examples.

Report status & verification

  • User-submitted reports start as Pending Review and are shown as unverified.
  • Only a reviewer can move them to Verified (counted as confirmed damage) or Resolved.
  • Pending reports do not count toward confirmed damage in the priority score.

Known limitations

  • No real elevation model; elevation is approximated from distance to a synthetic channel.
  • Facility and road coverage depends on OpenStreetMap completeness, which varies by area.
  • Scores are relative within a study area and should not be compared across cities.
  • If the network is unavailable, the app falls back to clearly-labelled simulated sample data.
  • Persistence: submissions are saved in this browser's localStorage only — not on a server, and they do not sync across devices.

Confidence & uncertainty indicators

Coverage indicator

Every zone explanation shows how many real facility records informed it. Fewer than 3 nearby facilities → low coverage, flagged in the explanation.

Missing-data handling

A zone with no confirmed damage reports is shown as "no confirmed damage data" and the damage term contributes 0 — it is never invented.

Simulated AI

The demo assessment outputs a severity and a confidence range, explicitly labelled as a brightness/texture heuristic, not a model.

Validation needed before operational use

Moving beyond this prototype would require: a real elevation/terrain model, calibrated drainage data, observed rainfall and waterlogging records, an independently labelled damage dataset to train and validate any computer-vision model, field verification of reports, and formal agreement with the responsible municipal and disaster-management authorities.

Full disclaimer

FloodLens AI is a decision-support demonstration and prototype. It is not an operational flood-warning system. It does not forecast flooding, does not issue alerts, and must not be used for evacuation, emergency response, or safety-critical decisions. All risk scores, rainfall scenarios, historical notes, road-damage severities and AI assessments are illustrative unless explicitly attributed to OpenStreetMap. Simulated values are never presented as real observations or as validated AI predictions. Always consult official meteorological, hydrological and municipal authorities.
